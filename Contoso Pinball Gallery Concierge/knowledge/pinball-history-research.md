# Contoso Pinball Gallery -- History and Research Reference

Background reference the Concierge uses to answer research and history questions about the titles Contoso carries and the eras they come from. Written to be broadly accurate to real pinball history, with Contoso's own showroom framing. When a customer asks "who made this" or "what era is this," this is the grounding.

Historical framing for course demos. Contoso Pinball Gallery is fictional.

---

## The eras of pinball, in plain terms

| Era | Rough years | Defining tech | Feel |
|-----|-------------|----------------|------|
| Electromechanical (EM) | pre-1978 | Relays, score reels, chimes | Mechanical, tactile, no CPU |
| Solid-state (SS) | 1977-1990 | Circuit boards, digital scoring, speech | Faster, rule-driven, early multiball |
| DMD (dot-matrix display) | 1991-1999 | 128x32 dot-matrix, deep rulesets | The "golden era" of design depth |
| LCD / modern | 2015-present | Color LCD, node boards, code updates | Cinematic, updatable, connected |

A quick way to place a machine: **chimes** means EM, **a scrolling dot-matrix animation** means DMD, **a full color screen** means modern.

---

## Manufacturers you will hear about

- **Williams** and **Bally** -- the two dominant names of the SS and DMD eras. Bally was absorbed into Williams; many "Bally" DMD titles are Williams-built. Williams left pinball in 1999.
- **Stern Pinball** -- the through-line to today. The last continuously operating major manufacturer, maker of every modern LCD title in the showroom (Godzilla, Jaws, Rush).
- **Gottlieb** and **Chicago Coin** -- major EM-era names, less represented in current inventory.

## Deep dives on showroom titles

### Medieval Madness -- Williams, 1997 (DMD)
Designed by Brian Eddy with software by Lyman Sheats. Widely ranked the greatest pinball machine ever made. The castle with a working drawbridge, the trolls that pop up from the playfield, and "Battle for the Kingdom" as the wizard mode. Its reputation is why original runs command a premium and why it was reissued years later. Contoso's is an **original 1997 run**, not a remake.

### Attack from Mars -- Bally, 1995 (DMD)
Also Brian Eddy, the design sibling to Medieval Madness. Faster and more aggressive, built around blowing up the mothership and the "Rule the Universe" wizard mode. Beloved for flow: shots feel fast and connected. If a customer loves Attack from Mars, point them at Medieval Madness, and vice versa.

### The Twilight Zone -- Bally, 1993 (DMD)
Pat Lawlor's ambitious masterpiece. The **Powerball** is a lighter ceramic ball that changes physics mid-game. Features a working gumball machine, a mini-playfield, and the Slot Machine. Famously deep and hard to master, which is exactly why enthusiasts love it.

### The Addams Family -- Bally, 1992 (DMD)
Also Pat Lawlor. The **best-selling solid-state pinball of all time** (about 20,000 units). Thing's hand grabs the ball, the Swamp and the Vault, and "The Power" magnet. The commercial high-water mark of the DMD era.

### Black Knight -- Williams, 1980 (SS)
Steve Ritchie's landmark. First machine with a **two-level playfield** and **magna-save**. Its speech and its menacing Black Knight callouts were cutting-edge for 1980. A foundational solid-state design.

### Firepower -- Williams, 1980 (SS)
The first **widebody** with **multiball** and **lane change**. A milestone that pushed solid-state rulesets forward. Contoso's is a **project-grade** restoration candidate, which makes it a good teaching example of what "sold as-is" means.

### Fireball -- Bally, 1972 (EM)
An electromechanical standout: **zipper flippers** that close the outlane gap, a spinning disc that whips the ball around, and a rare three-ball multiball for its era. Contoso's is a concours restoration with correct chimes -- a reference for what "Museum" grade means.

### Godzilla -- Stern, 2021 (LCD) and Jaws -- Stern, 2024 (LCD)
Both designed by **Keith Elwin**, the most acclaimed modern designer. Godzilla's collapsing-building mechanism and magna-grab, and Jaws's motorized shark and depth-gauge ramp, show what modern LCD-era machines do that older ones cannot: motorized toys, layered code, and updates over time.

## How to answer a research question well

1. Name the **title, manufacturer, and year** so the customer confirms you have the right machine.
2. Place it in an **era** and say what that era means in a sentence.
3. Add one or two **signature features** or the designer.
4. If relevant, suggest a **related title** in the showroom.
5. Ground it. If the customer asks something not covered here, say so and offer to research it or connect them with staff.
